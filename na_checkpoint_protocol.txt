# Matched Gaussian NA-Hutch++ checkpoint experiment

## Operator cases and stopping rule

Use the ten fixed operator cases in `anchor_norm_protocol.txt`, with their existing independent numerical trace and action checks. Use 24 new paired paths per case. The zero-based case index `i` and path index `j` give seed `9060000 + 1000*i + j`. Draw one standard Gaussian query stream of 256 vectors in advance. Count each queried column as one product. Every method reports on the original shared grid of 12, 16, 24, 32, 44, 64, 96, 128, 192 and 256 products, at relative radius target 0.05 and nominal total risk 0.05 per method. Replay through the cap. Each certificate stops at the first positive, finite, nonempty running intersection whose relative radius `(U-L)/(U+L)` is at most 0.05. The returned estimate is the harmonic center `2LU/(L+U)`. Compare the first stops and charge 256 products to non-stops in the restricted mean. Record completion counts, incorrect stops, pathwise reference exclusions, interruptions and paired wins, ties and losses. These are descriptive comparisons on fixed operators.

Use origin-anchored Nyström progress with stake 1/3, and origin-anchored frozen norm with stakes 1/3 and 1/2. Evaluate each using the existing closed form and its exact-model time-uniform risk guarantee. The shared stake isolates the choice of evidence. The 1/2 stake reproduces the main frozen norm setting. The deterministic full-sketch lower bound is available to both Nyström rules and to a secondary version of the Gaussian NA-Hutch++ certificate. A primary Gaussian NA-Hutch++ certificate uses the original rank sketch and held-out residual observations alone.

## Gaussian NA-Hutch++ point estimator

At total budget `m`, partition the shared prefix into `s=m/4` left sketch columns `S`, `r=m/2` right sketch columns `R`, and `h=m/4` residual columns `G`, in that order. These are the experimental proportions of Meyer et al. and the Gaussian draws in Jiang et al., Algorithm 2. The partition is deterministic at each report. Thus the residual columns are independent of both sketch matrices at that report. Reports can reuse products and change their roles. Let `Z=AR`, `W=AS`, `P=(SᵀZ)†`, and `M=Z P Wᵀ`. The published-form point estimate is

$$
\widehat\theta = \operatorname{tr}(M)+\frac1h\sum_{j=1}^h g_j^\top(A-M)g_j.
$$

The point estimate uses the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse in the exact model. The numerical implementation uses SciPy's default rank tolerance. Report fixed-budget point-estimator errors and relative RMSE separately from errors at the certificate's stopping time.

- Meyer, Musco, Musco and Woodruff, *Hutch++: Optimal Stochastic Trace Estimation*, Algorithm 2 and §6. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09649)
- Jiang, Pham, Woodruff and Zhang, *Optimal Sketching for Trace Estimation*, Algorithm 2. [Proceedings paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/c77bfda61a0204d445185053e6a9a8fe-Paper.pdf)

## Finite-sample certificate for the signed residual

Condition on `S,R`. Put $C=A-(M+M^\top)/2$. The trace error in the point estimator is the centered mean of $g_j^\top Cg_j$. The already counted products $Ag_j$ and low-rank factors of $M$ give $Cg_j$. The certificate uses signed symmetric Gaussian concentration and two observable norm bounds. Let $F=\|C\|_F$, $B=\|C\|_{\mathrm{op}}$, $V=\sum_j\|Cg_j\|_2^2$ and $H=[Cg_1,\ldots,Cg_h]$.

For $F>0$, $V/F^2$ has law $\sum_i w_i Y_i$ for independent $Y_i\sim\chi_h^2$ and $w_i=\lambda_i(C)^2/F^2$ summing to one. At $t>0$, convexity of $-\log(1+2tw)$ on $[0,1]$ gives

$$
\mathbb{E}e^{-t V/F^2}=\prod_i(1+2tw_i)^{-h/2}\le(1+2t)^{-h/2}.
$$

Taking $t=(1/u-1)/2$ gives $\Pr[V\le h u F^2]\le\exp[-h(u-1-\log u)/2]$ for $0<u<1$. If $u$ solves $h(u-1-\log u)/2=\log(1/\delta_F)$, then $F_U=\sqrt{V/(hu)}$ bounds $F$ with failure probability at most $\delta_F$. For a unit eigenvector $v$ attaining $B$, $\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\ge\|v^\top H\|_2^2=B^2\|v^\top G\|_2^2$ and $\|v^\top G\|_2^2\sim\chi_h^2$. With the lower $\delta_B$ quantile $q_h$, use $B_U=\min(F_U,\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\sqrt{q_h})$. The bounds hold jointly except on a set of conditional probability at most $\delta_F+\delta_B$. The case $C=0$ gives $F=B=V=0$.

For either sign of $C$ and $0<t<1/(2B)$, the signed Gaussian moment generating function gives

$$
\log\mathbb{E}\exp\!\left[t\sum_j(g_j^\top Cg_j-\operatorname{tr}C)\right]
=\frac h2\sum_i[-\log(1-2t\lambda_i)-2t\lambda_i]
\le\frac{ht^2F^2}{1-2tB}.
$$

The inequality sums the log series using absolute values and $1/k\le1/2$ for $k\ge2$. The Chernoff choice $t=\sqrt{x}/(\sqrt h F+2B\sqrt{x})$ yields the two-sided half-width $2F\sqrt{x/h}+2Bx/h$ with failure probability at most $2e^{-x}$. At each checkpoint assign $\delta=0.05/10$, with $\delta_F=\delta_B=\delta/4$ and $x=\log(4/\delta)$. Substitute $F_U,B_U$ for the two true norms. A union bound gives conditional failure probability at most $\delta$, even though the norm estimates and the quadratic observations use the same columns. Clipping the lower bound to zero uses PSD of $A$. Intersect the resulting intervals across the ten reports to obtain simultaneous coverage of at least 0.95 by another union bound. The secondary version also intersects the deterministic Nyström lower bound from all queried columns. Both certificate versions use the same risk event.

The certificate is attached to the Gaussian NA-Hutch++ point estimate for this comparison. The finite-sample derivation above supplies its calibration. The experiment uses binary64 products and numerical references, while the stochastic guarantee assumes exact arithmetic and Gaussian draws.
